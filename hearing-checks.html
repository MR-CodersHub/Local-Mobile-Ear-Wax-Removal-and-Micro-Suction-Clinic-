Early Warning Signs

Seven signs your ears are asking for help

Hearing change is gradual — these subtle signs often appear long before you notice the loss itself.

Turning up the TV

Volume creeping higher than it used to be is often the very first sign.

Struggling in groups

Noisy restaurants or family gatherings become exhausting to follow.

Phone conversations

Voices sound muffled, or you find yourself switching ears constantly.

New tinnitus

Ringing, buzzing or hissing — especially in quiet rooms — deserves assessment.

Ear fullness

A blocked or "cotton wool" feeling that does not shift after a cold.

Asking people to repeat

Frequent "sorry?" or "pardon?" in conversation is a classic early marker.

Withdrawal socially

Avoiding cafés, meetings or calls because listening is hard work.

Family history

Noise exposure at work or hereditary hearing loss — prevention starts with a baseline.

Your Assessment

Three tests, complete picture

A professional hearing check is more than pressing a button when you hear a beep. Here is what we measure.

Pure-Tone Audiometry

You wear headphones in a sound-treated booth and indicate when you hear tones at different pitches and volumes. The result — your audiogram — maps exactly what you can and cannot hear.

Tympanometry

A gentle pressure change tests how your eardrum moves, revealing fluid, perforations, Eustachian tube problems or glue ear that a tone test alone would miss.

Otoscopy & Wax Check

Before any test, we examine the ear canal with a camera. Wax or debris can mimic hearing loss — we clear the path first so your results are accurate.

The Experience

Forty-five minutes, zero stress

Your hearing check begins with a conversation — your lifestyle, your concerns, your work and hobbies. Context matters as much as decibels.

Then the three tests, each painless and relaxed. Finally, we sit down together and go through your results on screen, in plain English, with a printed report to take home.

  • Same-day results — no waiting weeks for a letter

Who Should Be Tested

Hearing checks aren't just for later life

Adults over 40

Hearing loss begins decades before symptoms appear. A baseline at 40 means changes are caught early — when they matter most.

Noise-exposed professionals

Musicians, construction workers and frequent flyers should test annually. We provide occupational hearing reports for employers.

Anyone with concerns

Tinnitus, fullness, dizziness or a family history of hearing loss — if something feels off, a 45-minute check gives you answers and peace of mind.
